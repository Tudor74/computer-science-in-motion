# Levin Lab In Motion: brief for era authors

Levin Lab In Motion runs on the shared In Motion engine, on branch `levin`, with sound available but rarely needed. It is a course on the research of Michael Levin's lab (the Allen Discovery Center at Tufts University) and the older work it builds on: membrane voltage in every cell, how an embryo tells left from right, regenerating planaria, frog faces, eyes and tails, cancer and brain development, xenobots, basal cognition, and the tools and open questions at the frontier. Follow the order of discovery within each era, and let each era build on the ones before it.

This course is about a living researcher's lab and much recent work. That sets its two main rules: **accuracy first** (say less rather than say something unchecked) and **visible evidence labels** (the learner can always tell a replicated finding from one lab's result, a framework, a speculation or a criticism).

The course is built around simplified models the learner can run and published results the learner can read beside them:
- one cell's voltage set by pumps and channels (a Goldman-type balance), then a sheet or ring of cells coupled by gap junctions, where the learner changes coupling and channel strengths and watches voltages spread, settle or split into domains
- the classic experiments as toy models: a ring of coupled cells carrying a left–right signal; a planarian fragment regrowing after a brief block of gap junctions, cut after cut; a voltage pattern painted on a frog embryo compared with the imaged "electric face"; a tail amputated before, during and after the refractory stage
- an evolutionary search in a toy physics world: random voxel bodies scored for movement, varied and kept, then "built" with noise to show what survives the gap between simulation and dish
- frameworks and debates set out as arguments the learner sorts: which claim is which kind, what evidence would change it, what critics say

Where the learner runs a model, draw the published result next to it and say plainly that the model is a cartoon of the mechanism, not the mechanism.

**Every number on screen is computed.** No voltage, ratio, count, percentage or fitness is typed into a scene as a literal: membrane voltages come from the scene's Goldman or Nernst calculation (or its network model) for the ion concentrations and permeabilities shown; coupled-cell voltages from the scene's own update rule; counts of heads, eyes or regrown tails from the toy model's outcome over seeded runs; xenobot fitness from the scene's simulator. The only literals allowed are published values (dates, concentrations used, reported percentages of worms or tadpoles affected, ion concentrations from a source) and they carry a `research` line naming the paper. When a published percentage is shown, show it as reported (with the paper's sample size if you are sure of it) and never re-derive it from the toy model.

## Evidence labels

Every scene's `tag` begins with one of these labels, and the drawing shows the same label as a small badge beside the claim it applies to (a scene that mixes kinds shows a badge on each part):

| Label | Use it for | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replicated finding** | results confirmed by several independent labs, or textbook physiology | resting potentials in non-excitable cells; gap junctions; steady currents at wounds and regenerating limbs; planarian regeneration from neoblasts; nodal flow in the mouse |
| **Single-lab finding** | results reported by the Levin lab and its collaborators, peer-reviewed but not yet widely replicated by independent groups | two-headed planarian persistence; species-specific heads; barium adaptation; memory after decapitation; induced eyes; KRAS-tumour suppression by hyperpolarisation; HCN2 brain rescue; xenobot kinematic replication; adult frog leg regrowth |
| **Framework or hypothesis** | the lab's theoretical proposals, offered as ways of seeing or as testable ideas | target morphology; the bioelectric code; TAME; the cognitive light cone; cancer as a shrinking of the self; navigating morphospace; competency at many scales |
| **Speculation** | philosophical ideas with no direct experimental test yet | a "Platonic space" of forms; mind as fundamental to biology at every scale |
| **Criticism and debate** | published criticism and live disagreement among scientists | cilia versus ion flux for left–right; anthropomorphism in basal cognition; whether xenobots are "robots" or "organisms"; how much of the lab's work has been independently tested |
| **Simplified model** | any simulation in the course, always, in addition to the label of the claim it illustrates | the toy bioelectric network; the gap-junction ring; the regrowth model; the voxel simulator |

Decide which label applies from the evidence, not from how well known a result is. If you are unsure whether a result has been independently replicated, use **Single-lab finding** and say in your report what you could not confirm. A result that the lab reports together with outside collaborators (for example the xenobot work with Josh Bongard's lab, or the leg regrowth with David Kaplan's lab) is still a single-lab finding in this sense until an independent group has repeated it. Neutral tone throughout: neither hype ("rewriting the rules of life", "cure") nor dismissal ("fringe", "pseudoscience"). Report what was done, what was found, and how strong the evidence is.

### Lesson notes

The papers below are given so that you can find and check them; they are not a licence to cite them unchecked. "Verify" means the brief is not sure of the detail (year, journal, author list, number): check it before using it, and leave it out if you cannot. Labels in brackets are the default label for the lesson's main claim.

**Era 1: bioelectricity before the lab**
- `membrane-voltage` [Replicated finding]: nearly every cell, not only neurons and muscle, keeps a voltage across its membrane, set by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and by potassium, sodium, chloride and other channels; Bernstein's membrane hypothesis (1902) for nerve; in non-excitable cells voltage changes slowly, over minutes to days, and differs between cell types (proliferating and stem cells tend to be depolarised, mature non-dividing cells hyperpolarised; Binggeli & Weinstein 1986, J Theor Biol, verify; Sundelacruz, Levin & Kaplan 2009, Stem Cell Reviews and Reports, verify). The learner sets concentrations and permeabilities and watches the computed voltage settle; then compares a "neuron" and a "skin cell" model, the first fast, the second slow. Link to Brain's `resting-potential`, `resting-potential-ghk` and `resting-potential-pump` and Biology's `membranes-pumps-cycle`.
- `early-bioelectricity` [Replicated finding for the measurements; Criticism and debate for the theories]: Galvani's frog experiments (*De viribus electricitatis*, 1791; link to Brain's `animal-electricity`), du Bois-Reymond's injury current (1840s, verify), Harold Saxton Burr and F. S. C. Northrop's "electro-dynamic theory of life" (Quarterly Review of Biology, 1935), Burr's "L-fields" and his voltage measurements on eggs, seeds and salamanders; Elmer J. Lund's *Bioelectric Fields and Growth* (1947). Say plainly that Burr's broader claims (fields as an organising blueprint) were not supported by the evidence of his time and were largely set aside, and that the field lost attention when molecular biology rose. The learner reads old voltage readings as plotted points (only published values, with research lines), not as a simulation.
- `vibrating-probe` [Replicated finding]: Jaffe & Nuccitelli (1974, J Cell Biol), the ultrasensitive vibrating probe for steady extracellular currents; Lionel Jaffe's work on fucoid eggs and polarity; Borgens, Vanable & Jaffe (1977, PNAS) on currents leaving the stumps of regenerating newt limbs; Borgens's later work on applied fields in frog limbs and spinal cord (verify specific claims before using; mark any human spinal-cord trials as clinical trials with limited results, not as treatments, or leave them out); Colin McCaig's lab on electric fields guiding cell migration and wound healing (McCaig, Rajnicek, Song & Zhao 2005, Physiological Reviews, verify) and Min Zhao's lab (Zhao et al. 2006, Nature, on electric fields directing wound healing; verify). The learner moves a simulated probe around a model cell and sees the computed current from its toy source and sink.
- `voltage-dyes` [Replicated finding]: oxonol dyes such as DiBAC4(3) collect more in depolarised cells and so report slow voltage differences; the lab's protocol (Adams & Levin 2012, Cold Spring Harbor Protocols, verify). Explain that the dye reports relative, not absolute, voltage, can be affected by other factors, and is slow; genetically encoded voltage indicators are an alternative. The learner paints a voltage pattern on a sheet of model cells and sees the computed "brightness" image; then sees that two quite different voltage maps can give similar images after blurring, a lesson in reading dye pictures.
- `gap-junctions` [Replicated finding]: gap junctions made of connexins (vertebrates) or innexins (invertebrates) join the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells; Furshpan & Potter (1959) found electrical coupling between neurons (link to Brain's `synapse-discovery-electrical`); Loewenstein's work on cell coupling (verify dates); gap junctions can be gated by voltage and pH. The learner couples a row of model cells, injects current into one, and watches the computed voltages spread and decay with distance; then lowers coupling and sees the cells become independent. This is the first toy bioelectric network of the course: label it **Simplified model**.
- `instructive-signals` [Framework or hypothesis]: a permissive signal allows a process; an instructive signal specifies which outcome. Wolpert's positional information and the French flag (link to Biology's `morphogenesis-flag`) and Turing's reaction–diffusion (Biology's `morphogenesis`) as the classic chemical accounts; the lab's argument that some bioelectric patterns are instructive, with the logic of the experiments that test it (changing voltage by several different channels with different ions and getting the same outcome suggests voltage itself, not one ion or protein, carries the signal). The learner sorts example experiments into "shows necessity", "shows sufficiency" and "shows instructiveness".

**Era 2: left and right**
- `body-asymmetry` [Replicated finding]: vertebrates are outwardly near-symmetric but inwardly asymmetric (heart, gut, liver, lungs); situs inversus and heterotaxy; laterality defects as experimental readouts (embryos are scored for heart, gut and gall-bladder position). The learner scores model embryos as normal, reversed or heterotaxic, and the scene computes the percentages; explain why a randomising treatment gives roughly half reversed and some heterotaxic, while a weak one raises heterotaxy only slightly. Keep any human medical content to one plain sentence: situs inversus by itself often causes no problems; heterotaxy can involve heart defects.
- `left-right-cascade` [Replicated finding]: Levin, Johnson, Stern, Kuehn & Tabin (1995, Cell), "A molecular pathway determining left-right asymmetry in chick embryogenesis": Sonic hedgehog on the left of Hensen's node, activin receptor IIa on the right, a Nodal-related gene (cNR-1) in left lateral plate mesoderm; moving these signals changes heart looping. Mention that Levin was a graduate student in Tabin's lab at Harvard. Later work by many labs built the Nodal–Lefty–Pitx2 cascade (verify which papers you cite). The learner switches on genes in order down one side of a model embryo and sees the computed heart-looping outcome.
- `ion-flux` [Single-lab finding, partly confirmed elsewhere; verify]: Levin, Thorlin, Robinson, Nogi & Mercola (2002, Cell), "Asymmetries in H+/K+-ATPase and cell membrane potentials comprise a very early step in left-right patterning", in frog and chick. Say which parts other labs have tested if you can confirm it; otherwise say it was reported by this group. The learner places pump molecules unevenly in a model 4-cell embryo and sees a computed voltage difference between left and right.
- `serotonin-flow` [Single-lab finding; Framework or hypothesis for the full model]: Fukumoto, Kema & Levin (2005, Current Biology) on serotonin as a very early left–right signal in chick and frog, and Fukumoto, Blakely & Levin (2005, Developmental Neuroscience, verify) on serotonin transporter; the proposed electrophoresis of serotonin through gap junctions toward one side. The learner runs a toy electrophoresis model: a charged molecule in a row of coupled cells drifts toward one end when a voltage gradient is set; label both the model and the mechanism.
- `gap-junction-model` [Single-lab finding; Framework or hypothesis]: Levin & Mercola (1998, Developmental Biology) in frog; Levin & Mercola (1999, Development) in chick; the model of a ring of coupled cells with a zone of low coupling. The learner breaks the ring at different places and sees the computed sidedness change. Label as **Simplified model**.
- `cilia-debate` [Criticism and debate]: Nonaka et al. (1998, Cell) on leftward nodal flow from motile cilia in mice (KIF3B mutants); Nonaka et al. (2002, Nature, verify) reversing the flow artificially reverses sidedness; McGrath, Somlo, Makova, Tian & Brueckner (2003, Cell, verify) on two cilia populations; the argument over whether ion flux acts upstream of cilia in frog and chick, whether the chick uses nodal cilia at all (Gros, Feistel, Viebahn, Blum & Tabin 2009, Science, on cell movements at Hensen's node, verify), Martin Blum's lab on cilia-driven flow in frog and rabbit (verify), and the lab's own positions, (Vandenberg & Levin 2013, Developmental Biology, verify). Present both views in their own words and do not adjudicate: say that the field has not settled how universal either mechanism is.

**Era 3: planaria**
- `worm-regeneration` [Replicated finding]: Thomas Hunt Morgan (1898, Archiv für Entwicklungsmechanik der Organismen) on small fragments regrowing whole worms (his much-quoted figure of a 1/279 fragment: verify before using); Harriet Randolph's earlier work (1897, verify); neoblasts as the dividing adult stem cells; Wagner, Wang & Reddien (2011, Science) showing a single clonogenic neoblast can restore a lethally irradiated worm; Alejandro Sánchez Alvarado's lab and Peter Reddien's lab as leading independent planarian groups (Wnt/β-catenin signalling sets head versus tail: Petersen & Reddien 2008; Gurley, Rink & Sánchez Alvarado 2008; Iglesias et al. 2008, all Science, verify). The learner cuts a model worm into pieces and sees each piece's computed head–tail polarity (a toy Wnt gradient) decide what it regrows.
- `two-headed-worms` [Single-lab finding]: Nogi & Levin (2005, Developmental Biology) on innexins and gap-junctional communication in planarian regeneration; Oviedo et al. (2010, Developmental Biology), "Long-range neural and gap junction protein-mediated cues control polarity during planarian regeneration" (check the full author list before giving it). Briefly soaking cut fragments in octanol, a gap-junction blocker, makes some regrow a head at both ends. Give the reported fraction only from the paper. The learner runs a toy regrowth model: block coupling for a set time, cut, and see the computed outcome over many seeded fragments. Note that the Wnt pathway work (above) shows a molecular route to two-headed worms too, and that the two accounts are being connected.
- `rewritten-pattern` [Single-lab finding; Framework or hypothesis for "target morphology"]: Oviedo et al. (2010) reported that two-headed worms, cut again in plain water with no further drug, again regrow two heads; Durant, Morokuma, Fields, Williams, Adams & Levin (2017, Biophysical Journal), "Long-term, stochastic editing of regenerative anatomy via targeting endogenous bioelectric gradients": some treated worms that look normal ("cryptic") regrow two heads when cut, and the change can be reversed by a further bioelectric treatment (verify the reversal detail). The "target morphology" idea: tissues work towards a stored pattern, and that pattern can be rewritten without editing the genome. Label the idea as a framework. The learner cuts the toy worm again and again and sees whether the stored pattern persists; label the toy as a simplified model of the lab's interpretation, not of a known molecular mechanism.
- `species-heads` [Single-lab finding]: Emmons-Bell et al. (2015, International Journal of Molecular Sciences), "Gap junctional blockade stochastically induces different species-specific head anatomies in genetically wild-type Girardia dorotocephala flatworms" (verify authors); heads resembling those of other species (including in brain shape and stem-cell distribution, verify), which later remodelled back (verify). The learner picks a coupling strength in a toy model with several stable head shapes and sees which "attractor" a fragment falls into; label as a simplified model of the lab's attractor interpretation.
- `barium-heads` [Single-lab finding]: Emmons-Bell et al. (2019, Communications Biology, verify), "Regenerative adaptation to electrochemical perturbation in planaria: a molecular analysis of physiological plasticity": barium chloride, a broad potassium-channel blocker, makes heads degenerate; worms left in barium regrow heads insensitive to it, with a small set of altered gene transcripts. The learner runs a toy cell that compensates for a blocked channel by changing other channel strengths until its computed voltage returns to target; label as a simplified model of homeostasis, not of the actual gene changes.
- `headless-memory` [Single-lab finding; Criticism and debate for the history]: James McConnell's 1950s–60s planarian conditioning and "memory transfer" experiments (McConnell, Jacobson & Kimble 1959, J Comp Physiol Psychol, verify) and why they were widely doubted (difficulty replicating, problems with controls); Shomrat & Levin (2013, Journal of Experimental Biology), "An automated training paradigm reveals long-term memory in planarians and its persistence through head regeneration": worms familiarised with a rough-floored arena later fed sooner there, and fragments that regrew heads showed savings after brief retraining. Say that this is one lab's result, that the effect is modest and needs retraining to appear, and that independent replication is needed (search for any later replication before writing; if none confirmed, say "not yet independently replicated" without inventing failures). No claim that memories are "stored in the tail" as an established fact.

**Era 4: frog faces, eyes and tails**
Introduce *Xenopus laevis*, the African clawed frog, as a standard laboratory animal: embryos develop outside the mother, are large and easy to inject; tadpoles regrow tails; adults do not regrow legs (only a cartilage spike).
- `electric-face` [Single-lab finding]: Vandenberg, Morrie & Adams (2011, Developmental Dynamics), "V-ATPase-dependent ectodermal voltage and pH regionalization are required for craniofacial morphogenesis": a voltage-dye time-lapse of the frog embryo's surface showing a hyperpolarised and depolarised pattern that prefigures eyes, mouth and other face structures; disrupting it alters gene expression and face shape. Credit Dany Spencer Adams and Laura Vandenberg. The video was widely reported as the "electric face"; do not describe the face as "glowing" in a way that suggests visible light from the embryo. The learner aligns a drawn voltage pattern with later face structures in a schematic; use a schematic, not an invented image of the published data.
- `induced-eyes` [Single-lab finding]: Pai, Aw, Shomrat, Lemire & Levin (2012, Development), "Transmembrane voltage potential controls embryonic eye patterning in Xenopus laevis": cells set to a particular voltage range, by misexpressing ion channels, formed eye-like structures in gut, tail and elsewhere, recruiting neighbouring cells; they involved eye-field genes (verify which). Give the voltage range only from the paper. The learner sets a model cell's channels and sees whether its computed voltage falls in the reported range (shown as a published band).
- `tail-regeneration` [Replicated finding for regeneration and the refractory period; Single-lab finding for the V-ATPase rescue]: Beck, Christen & Slack (2003, Developmental Cell) on the refractory period in *Xenopus* tail regeneration and its rescue by BMP or Notch; Adams, Masi & Levin (2007, Development), "H+ pump-dependent changes in membrane voltage are an early mechanism necessary and sufficient to induce Xenopus tail regeneration" (expressing a yeast proton pump, PMA1, restored regrowth in the refractory stage; verify). The learner amputates a model tail at different stages and with a pump switched on or off and sees the computed outcome.
- `tail-sodium` [Single-lab finding]: Tseng, Beane, Lemire, Masi & Levin (2010, Journal of Neuroscience), "Induction of vertebrate regeneration by a transient sodium current": the sodium channel NaV1.2 and the importance of sodium entry; a one-hour treatment with the sodium ionophore monensin plus raised sodium triggered regrowth in the refractory stage (verify these details). The learner sets the duration of a pulse in a toy model and sees the computed threshold behaviour; say that a toy threshold is not the paper's mechanism.
- `picasso-tadpoles` [Single-lab finding; Framework or hypothesis for its interpretation]: Vandenberg, Adams & Levin (2012, Developmental Dynamics), "Normalized shape and location of perturbed craniofacial structures in the Xenopus tadpole reveal an innate ability to achieve correct morphology": tadpoles with displaced face organs (from early perturbations) often moved them into near-normal positions during metamorphosis. The "Picasso tadpole" name is the lab's. The learner drags model eyes and jaws into odd places and watches a toy "error-reducing" model move them toward a target; contrast with a "hardwired movement" model that cannot correct, and say which the data favoured and what other explanations are possible.
- `leg-regrowth` [Single-lab finding]: Murugan et al. (2022, Science Advances), "Acute multidrug delivery via a wearable bioreactor facilitates long-term limb regeneration and functional recovery in adult Xenopus laevis" (verify the author list), with David Kaplan's lab at Tufts: a silicone cap (BioDome) holding a silk hydrogel with five compounds for 24 hours, then about 18 months of growth (verify), giving a longer, more patterned regrowth with some function than controls, but not a normal leg. Mention the lab's earlier progesterone study (Herrera-Rincon et al. 2018, Cell Reports, verify). Say plainly: this is an animal study in frogs; it is not a human treatment and makes no claim about humans. Mark company spin-offs and press coverage as out of scope.

**Era 5: cancer and the brain**
"Not medical advice" appears in every scene of this era, in the scene text, and no scene suggests that any bioelectric approach treats cancer or brain disorders in people.
- `depolarised-cells` [Replicated finding for the correlation; Framework or hypothesis for causation]: Clarence D. Cone (1971, Journal of Theoretical Biology), "Unified theory on the basic mechanism of normal mitotic control and oncogenesis", and his experiments on voltage and division (verify); Binggeli & Weinstein (1986, J Theor Biol) on membrane potentials and proliferation; many ion channels are altered in cancers (for example reviews by Prevarskaya, Skryma & Shuba 2018, Physiological Reviews, or Pardo & Stühmer 2014, Nature Reviews Cancer: verify). The learner moves a model cell's voltage and sees the published correlation drawn as a band, with the caveat that correlation does not show that voltage drives division.
- `instructor-cells` [Single-lab finding]: Blackiston, Adams, Lemire, Lobikin & Levin (2011, Disease Models & Mechanisms), "Transmembrane potential of GlyCl-expressing instructor cells induces a neoplastic-like conversion of melanocytes via a serotonergic pathway": depolarising a sparse set of cells (by a glycine-gated chloride channel and ivermectin) made melanocytes across the tadpole hyperproliferate, change shape and invade; Lobikin et al. (2012, Physical Biology, verify) on the all-or-none, stochastic response. Describe the tadpoles plainly; no images of distress. The learner sets how many instructor cells are depolarised in a toy model and sees the computed fraction of "converted" animals.
- `kras-tumours` [Single-lab finding]: Chernet & Levin (2013, Disease Models & Mechanisms), "Transmembrane voltage potential is an essential cellular parameter for the detection and control of tumor development in a Xenopus model": tumour-like structures induced by mutant human KRAS (and other oncogenes, verify) were depolarised in voltage-dye images, and hyperpolarising cells by misexpressing ion channels reduced their incidence; follow-up on butyrate and the mechanism (Chernet & Levin 2014, Oncotarget, verify). These are induced tumour-like structures in tadpoles, not human cancers. The learner toggles a model's hyperpolarising channel and sees the computed incidence over seeded runs, with the published incidence drawn separately as a labelled value.
- `cell-coupling` [Replicated finding for reduced coupling in many tumours; Criticism and debate for its role]: Loewenstein & Kanno (1966, Nature), "Intercellular communication and the control of tissue growth: lack of communication between cancer cells"; James Trosko's work on gap-junctional communication and tumour promotion (verify); connexins as both tumour suppressors and, in some cancers, promoters of spread (cite a review only if checked). The learner couples and uncouples a sheet of model cells and watches whether a depolarised patch is pulled back to the tissue voltage or escapes.
- `brain-rescue` [Single-lab finding]: Pai, Lemire, Paré, Lin, Chen & Levin (2015, Journal of Neuroscience), "Endogenous gradients of resting potential instructively pattern embryonic neural tissue via Notch signaling and regulation of proliferation"; Pai et al. (2018, Nature Communications), "HCN2 Rescues brain defects by enforcing endogenous voltage pre-patterns" (check the author list): in frog embryos with brain defects from nicotine or mutant Notch, raising HCN2 channel activity (including with the drug lamotrigine, verify) restored more normal brain anatomy and some learning in a tadpole assay; a computational model (with Pietak) guided the choice. Say plainly: frog embryos; no human relevance claimed here; no advice about any drug. The learner adjusts a toy coupled-cell model to restore a pre-pattern and sees the computed pattern match.
- `boundary-of-self` [Framework or hypothesis]: Levin's proposal that cancer is cells disconnecting from the tissue's bioelectric network and shrinking their "self" back to the single cell (Levin 2021, Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology, "Bioelectrical approaches to cancer as a problem of the scaling of the cellular self", verify; also Levin 2019 on the "computational boundary of a self"). Present it as a hypothesis with the experiments offered for it (era 5 above) and what would test it, and give the standard view (somatic mutation theory) and other views (for example the tissue organisation field theory of Sonnenschein and Soto, verify) fairly. No suggestion that it changes cancer treatment.

**Era 6: xenobots**
- `computer-designed` [Single-lab finding, with collaborators]: Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin & Bongard (2020, PNAS), "A scalable pipeline for designing reconfigurable organisms": an evolutionary algorithm searched body shapes of passive (skin) and contractile (heart-muscle) voxels in a physics simulator; some designs were built by hand from *Xenopus laevis* cells (by Douglas Blackiston, using microsurgery) and moved. Credit Sam Kriegman and Josh Bongard (University of Vermont). The word "xenobot" comes from *Xenopus* plus "robot".
- `evolved-designs` [Simplified model; Replicated finding for evolutionary design as a method]: Karl Sims's evolved virtual creatures (1994, verify venue) and Lipson & Pollack (2000, Nature, "Automatic design and manufacture of robotic lifeforms") as forerunners; the 2020 pipeline's steps (random bodies, scoring, variation, selection, a filter for robustness to noise and buildability, verify). The learner runs a small evolutionary search in a 2D toy voxel world (seeded), watches computed fitness rise, then "builds" the best design with noise and sees how much fitness is lost: the gap between simulation and reality. Label the toy simulator clearly; it is not the paper's simulator.
- `ciliated-xenobots` [Single-lab finding]: Blackiston, Lederer, Kriegman, Garnier, Bongard & Levin (2021, Science Robotics), "A cellular platform for the development of synthetic living machines": skin progenitor cells self-assembled into spheroids that grew motile cilia on the outside and swam; they could heal, work in groups, and carry a "memory" written with a photoconvertible fluorescent protein (EosFP, green to red after blue light; verify). The learner steers a model spheroid by changing the computed cilia beat pattern.
- `self-replication` [Single-lab finding]: Kriegman, Blackiston, Levin & Bongard (2021, PNAS), "Kinematic self-replication in reconfigurable organisms": swarms of spheroids pushed loose dissociated frog cells into piles that matured into new spheroids; a C-shaped ("Pac-Man") design found in simulation increased the number of generations (verify how many). Explain "kinematic" (by movement and gathering) as different from replication by growth or by copying a genome; the offspring did not grow from the parents' bodies. The learner runs a toy 2D model of pushers and loose cells and counts computed piles per generation.
- `anthrobots` [Single-lab finding]: Gumuskaya, Srivastava, Manning, Blackiston & Levin (Advanced Science; published online late 2023, in the 2024 volume, verify), "Motile living biobots self-construct from adult human somatic progenitor seed cells": adult human tracheal (airway) cells grown as organoids with cilia outward moved in several distinct patterns, and in a dish encouraged neurons to grow across a scratch in a neural monolayer. Say: dish experiments; no claim of medical use. The learner sorts model anthrobot tracks into the paper's movement types (only if the categories are confirmed from the paper).
- `organism-or-robot` [Criticism and debate]: the first xenobots were genetically unmodified frog cells with no nervous system, could not feed, and lived days to weeks on stored yolk before breaking down (verify the lifespan figures). Whether "organism", "robot", "machine" or "living material" is right is a matter of definition. Press coverage ("living robots", "self-replicating robots") overstated some points; give the scientists' own wording where you can confirm it. Ethics and risk: published commentary on biobots, biosafety, moral status and governance (cite only pieces you have found and read, for example in bioethics or robotics journals). Present at least two views and do not take sides.

**Era 7: basal cognition** (every scene in this era is labelled **Framework or hypothesis** or **Criticism and debate**, except reports of specific experiments)
- `basal-cognition` [Replicated finding for the behaviours; Framework for the field's view]: Herbert Spencer Jennings, *Behavior of the Lower Organisms* (1906), on *Stentor* and other single cells, and a modern replication of Stentor's avoidance hierarchy (Dexter, Prabakaran & Gunawardena 2019, Current Biology, verify); slime moulds solving mazes and building efficient networks (Nakagaki, Yamada & Tóth 2000, Nature; Tero et al. 2010, Science, verify); bacterial chemotaxis as sensing and memory; Pamela Lyon's "biogenic approach" (Lyon 2006, Cognitive Processing) and the theme issue edited by Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt & Levin (2021, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, "Basal cognition: multicellularity, neurons and the cognitive lens"; verify title); Baluška & Levin (2016, Frontiers in Psychology, verify). Credit Pamela Lyon. The learner runs a toy chemotaxis model with and without a memory term and sees the computed path.
- `tame-framework` [Framework or hypothesis]: Levin (2022, Frontiers in Systems Neuroscience), "Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere: an experimentally-grounded framework for understanding diverse bodies and minds": cognition as a continuum; an "axis of persuadability" from systems best changed by rewiring to systems best changed by reasons; competencies as an empirical question. Present it as a proposal with its claimed advantages and the questions it raises. The learner places example systems (a thermostat, a bacterium, a planarian, a dog, a person) on the axis and sees which kind of intervention each placement implies; there is no right answer key, only the framework's suggested placements, labelled as such.
- `cognitive-light-cone` [Framework or hypothesis]: Levin (2019, Frontiers in Psychology), "The computational boundary of a self: developmental bioelectricity drives multicellularity and scale-free cognition": the spatial and temporal extent of the largest goal a system can pursue, drawn as a cone; cells joining into tissues scaling up the cone. Say it is a diagram for comparison, with no agreed way to measure it. The learner drags a system's cone wider and longer and reads the framework's description of what that would mean.
- `morphospace-navigation` [Framework or hypothesis]: Fields & Levin (2022, Entropy), "Competency in navigating arbitrary spaces as an invariant for analyzing cognition in diverse embodiments"; William James's definition of intelligence as reaching the same goal by different means (verify the quotation before using it; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks); regeneration and the Picasso tadpoles (era 4) as examples offered by the lab. The learner moves a point through a toy two-dimensional "shape space" with obstacles, and compares a fixed-recipe path with a goal-seeking one; label the toy and the analogy.
- `gene-network-learning` [Single-lab finding in computer models; Framework for its significance]: Biswas, Manicka, Hoel & Levin (2021, iScience), "Gene regulatory networks exhibit several kinds of memory: quantification of memory in biological and random transcriptional networks"; Biswas, Clawson & Levin (2023, International Journal of Molecular Sciences, verify) on learning in transcriptional network models and pathway-level memory. Say that these are simulations of published network models, and that whether real cells show associative learning in their gene networks is an open experimental question (mention any laboratory test only if you have confirmed the paper). The learner pairs two stimuli in a small toy network and sees whether its computed response to one stimulus alone changes; label it as a simplified model.
- `cognition-debate` [Criticism and debate]: published criticism of extending "cognition" to cells and tissues: risk of anthropomorphism; terms stretched until they lose explanatory force; the definitional disagreement (for example Adams & Aizawa on the "mark of the cognitive", or Adams 2018 on "cognition wars", verify); critics of "plant intelligence" (Alpi et al. 2007, Trends in Plant Science, verify) as a parallel debate; replies from the basal-cognition side. Present it as an open argument; give at least two named positions with real citations; no strawmen and no straw replies. Link to Consciousness In Motion's `animal-consciousness`, `sentience-markers` and `illusionism-panpsychism-combination`, and to Mind In Motion's `embodied-cognition`, and say plainly that basal cognition is about measurable behaviour, not a claim that cells are conscious.

## Levin Lab rules

### Accuracy and verification
- Cite a paper (authors, year, journal, title) only when you are confident it exists and says what you say. Use the WebSearch tool, and the web-fetch agent, when they are available, to check each citation and each specific number before you use it: the journal page, PubMed or the lab's publication list. If you cannot confirm a detail, say less: drop the number, give the decade instead of the year, or leave the paper out.
- The notes above mark many details "verify". Treat every author list, year and number in this brief as a lead, not as a confirmed fact.
- **No invented quotes.** Quote someone only with words you have read in the source, and give the source. Otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks.
- Do not attribute views to other scientists without a published source. Do not invent criticisms or failed replications; do not invent endorsements either.
- Claims from 2025–2026: include them only if you are certain of the source; otherwise leave them out. Prefer the original peer-reviewed paper to press releases, news stories, talks or podcasts; where a talk or blog post is the only source (as may be true for some of `platonic-space`), say so.
- The lab publishes a great deal. Choose a few central papers per lesson, and say what each showed; do not pad research lines.

### Evidence labels
See **Evidence labels** above. Every scene's `tag` starts with its label, and every drawing shows a badge with the label beside the claim. A scene's `definition` or `example` says the label in words where the claim is made ("reported by one lab, not yet independently replicated"; "a hypothesis"; "speculation"). The lab's own frameworks (TAME, the cognitive light cone, target morphology, the bioelectric code, cancer as a shrinking of the self, morphological problem space) are always **Framework or hypothesis**; the "Platonic space" of forms and similar philosophical ideas are always **Speculation**.

### Simplified models, computed numbers
- Every simulation (one cell's voltage, a gap-junction network, a ring of coupled cells, a regrowth toy, a voxel simulator, a gene network) carries the **Simplified model** badge and a sentence saying what it leaves out.
