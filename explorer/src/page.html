        Ethics and limits

        What these signals cannot do

        Matthew effect
        Track record favours established labs. A strong record raises a paper's signal, so well-known groups gain and new voices start lower. That is why newcomers sit in their own lane and are never ranked.
        Bias
        Publication and citation are shaped by field, gender and geography. Some fields publish far less in journals, citation gaps by gender are well documented, and authors outside North America and Europe are under-indexed. These signals inherit all of it; field averages only partly correct for it.
        Correlation, not truth
        The signals correlate (weakly, see the backtest) with later outcomes: being published, not retracted, still cited. Those outcomes are not truth. BICEP2 was published and is still cited; OPERA came from a superb collaboration and was wrong.
        Missing records
        Many good papers are never formally published: mathematics and computer science often stop at arXiv or a conference, and some results are simply complete as preprints. “Not published” is not “wrong”.
        Data gaps
        OpenAlex and Crossref are incomplete: preprint and journal versions are not always linked, author identities are sometimes split or merged, retractions are recorded late, and very new preprints may not be indexed yet.
        No naming and shaming
        Individuals are never ranked. Author track records appear only as pooled, aggregate signals on a paper's card (first and last authors together), never as an author leaderboard, and no author-level numbers are published here.

        Method and weekly re-run

        How the snapshot is built

        python3 explorer/build_snapshot.py   # past 7 days → explorer/data/snapshot-YYYY-MM-DD.json
python3 explorer/backtest.py         # optional: refresh the 2018–21 backtest
python3 explorer/build_page.py       # embeds the newest snapshot → explorer/index.html
node explorer/test/check.js          # Playwright checks and screenshots

        Run it once a week (for example every Tuesday). Keep the previous snapshot file in explorer/data/: the “status changed” filter compares against it. Responses are cached in explorer/cache/, which is not committed. Problems and their keyword rules live in explorer/problems.json.
